No CAN cable
between the ESCs.

Two independent VESC-compatible GaN motor channels on a 99 × 99 mm board. Each keeps its own STM32, USB-C port and three-shunt sensing; an ESP32 joins both on an internal CAN bus for coordinated traction control.

01The brief

A two-wheel-drive light EV normally gets two separate ESCs and the wiring between them. Twin-GaN puts both controllers on one 99 × 99 mm PCB to reduce board count, size and cost. The trade-off is a lower peak-power rating in exchange for the integration.

Each channel keeps an independent STM32F405, USB-C port and three-shunt sensing. An ESP32-C3 provides a third CAN node alongside the two motor controllers.

03Why one board

One CAN bus, three nodes. Both STM32s and the ESP32 sit on the same terminated internal CAN bus. Torque and control information can move between channels without external wiring, giving future traction-control firmware somewhere to start.

VESC Tool still sees two controllers. Each channel has its own MCU and six-PWM stage, so setup stays familiar and no custom combined-control interface is needed.

Three shunts per channel. Each phase uses an INA240A1D across a 2 mΩ shunt. REF1 and REF2 are connected for bidirectional measurement, so the controller can distinguish drive current from regeneration.

The usual connections are already there. Each controller gets USB-C with CC pulldowns and VBUS protection, Tag-Connect SWD and the standard VESC accessory header. No adapter-cable hunt is required for basic commissioning.

04Where it stands

R1 is at the design stage. The 60 V input, 20 A total battery current, 20 A continuous and 40 A absolute-max phase current per channel, and roughly 1.2 kW total are targets awaiting bench tests. Until then, they describe what the board is intended to survive, not what it has already survived.

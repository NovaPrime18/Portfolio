Work / 05 Side project

Two motor drives,
one 99 mm board.

A dual VESC-compatible controller with two complete three-phase GaN inverters on a 99 × 99 mm board. Both STM32s and an ESP32 share one internal CAN bus, allowing the motor channels to coordinate directly for traction control.

01The brief

Two-wheel-drive light electric vehicles often need two motor controllers. Twin-GaN puts both channels on one 99 × 99 mm board, with independent STM32F405 controllers, USB-C ports and three-shunt sensing. An ESP32-C3 provides a third CAN node.

The aim is to reduce size and cost compared with two separate ESCs, accepting a lower peak-power rating in exchange for integration.

03Why one board

One CAN bus, three nodes. Both STM32s and the ESP32 share one terminated internal CAN bus, allowing the channels to exchange torque and control information without external wiring. This provides a hardware basis for on-board traction control.

Two VESC targets. Each channel has its own MCU and six-PWM stage, so VESC Tool detects and configures them as two separate controllers. That keeps the familiar setup workflow and avoids a custom control interface.

Three-shunt sensing per channel. Each phase uses an INA240A1D across a 2 mΩ shunt. REF1 and REF2 are connected for bidirectional measurement, so the controller can distinguish drive and regenerative current.

Practical connections. Each controller has its own USB-C port with CC pulldowns and VBUS protection, plus Tag-Connect SWD and the standard VESC accessory header. These details should make programming, commissioning and future expansion straightforward.

04Where it stands

R1 is at the design stage. The targets are 60 V input, 20 A battery current, 20 A continuous and 40 A absolute-max phase current per channel, and roughly 1.2 kW total. None of these figures has been bench-verified. They are labelled as targets here and in the repository so the next person to build the board knows what still needs testing.

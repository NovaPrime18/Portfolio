Work / 06 Where I started

The thing that
got me here.

A battery-powered soundboard with 36 Cherry MX switches, a vacuum fluorescent display and stereo speakers. It is also a macro-pad. And a calculator. It was the first board I ever finished, and it is the reason there are five more.

01What it is

A device whose entire job is to play dumb sounds when you press a button. Thirty-six Cherry MX-style switches, a vacuum fluorescent display, stereo speakers, a battery, USB-C charging, and a power-path manager that runs from USB, battery, or a mix of both without anyone noticing the transition.

It runs in three configurations. As a soundboard, each key plays a file straight from the SD card. Plugged into a computer over USB-C, it becomes a 36-key macro-pad with assignable macros. Flip it into the third mode and it is a fully functional basic calculator — because a device with a VFD and a keypad that cannot do arithmetic is leaving value on the table.

03Why it matters

Every later board on this site owes something to this one. It taught the loop that actually matters: pick a thing that does not need to exist, then make it exist properly. Power path, battery charging, keyboard matrix, display driving, audio output, enclosure, firmware, and the specific misery of a silkscreen that looked fine in the viewer and unreadable on the physical board.

The over-engineering is the point. Nobody needs 36 buttons to play a sound. Building it anyway is how you find out what a board feels like when it is finished, and that is a different skill from designing one.
